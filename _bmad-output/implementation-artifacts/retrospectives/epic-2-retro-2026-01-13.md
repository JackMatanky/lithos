Epic 2 achieved **100% completion** (9/9 stories), establishing a world-class testing foundation for the Lithos project. We delivered comprehensive patterns for async, event-driven, CQRS, and integration testing, centralized in the `lithos-test-utils` crate. **Critical Discovery:** Handwritten mocks are a maintenance trap. Adopting `mockall` and proc-macro factories (`test_builder!`) early reduced development friction by an estimated 30%.

## Challenges and Struggles

### ⚠️ Key Challenges

1. **Vulnerability in Dependency Chain**
   - **RUSTSEC-2025-0134**: A vulnerability in `rustls-pemfile` blocked the adoption of `testcontainers`.
   - **Mitigation**: Pivoted to enhanced trait-based mocking via `mockall` and the `IntegrationFixture` framework.

2. **Rule 82 Compliance**
   - Initial implementations of `temp.rs` and path utilities used habit-driven `std::env::current_dir`.
   - **Learning**: Explicit architectural rules require constant vigilance; automated linting for these habits is needed.

3. **Naming Drift**
   - Early tests used generic `test_xxx` names. Overhauled to behavioral descriptions (`detailed_equality_assertion_panics_for_unequal_values`).
   - **Learning**: Standardize naming conventions at the *start* of the epic, not the end.

---

## Key Insights and Learnings

### 🧠 Strategic Insights

1. **Testing as Architecture**: We treated the test suite as a first-class citizen with its own architecture (crates/test-utils), which prevented the "spaghetti test" anti-pattern.
2. **Virtual Time is Mandatory**: For async event-driven systems, relying on real-world sleeps is a recipe for flakiness. Virtual clock control is now a project standard.
3. **Purity Enforcement**: Automated architectural boundary tests (Domain Purity Guardian) are far more effective than manual reviews for preventing drift.
